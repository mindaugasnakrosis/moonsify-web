Is 7 mg of Melatonin Right for You? A Practical Dosage Guide

By the Moonsify Team · Published August 13, 2026 · 6 min read

Melatonin supplements range from 0.3 mg microdoses to 20 mg mega-doses, and the internet will happily argue for every number in between. So how much melatonin should you actually take? Here's what the research says — and why the delivery method changes the answer.

Key takeaways

  • Common effective doses in research range from 0.5 mg to 10 mg; more is not automatically better.
  • Timing matters as much as dose — melatonin taken 30–60 minutes before your target bedtime works with your body clock, not against it.
  • Oral melatonin's bioavailability is low and variable (~15%), so the swallowed number overstates what reaches your bloodstream.
  • Transdermal patches spread delivery across the night instead of dosing it all at once.
  • Start low, stay consistent, and talk to your doctor if you take medication or are pregnant or nursing.

What the research actually shows

A widely cited meta-analysis of 19 randomized, placebo-controlled trials found that melatonin helped adults fall asleep about 7 minutes faster and sleep about 8 minutes longer on average, with improved overall sleep quality.1 Those are modest averages across many different doses and populations — individual responses vary a lot.

Interestingly, research hasn't shown a clean "more is better" curve. Studies using low doses (0.5–3 mg) often perform comparably to higher ones for sleep onset, because melatonin works primarily as a signal to your circadian system rather than as a sedative. It tells your brain "it's night now" — shouting the message louder doesn't necessarily make it clearer.

Why the milligrams on the label aren't what your body gets

When melatonin is swallowed, it passes through the liver before reaching circulation, and much of it is metabolized on the way. A systematic review of melatonin pharmacokinetics found absolute oral bioavailability of roughly 15%, varying widely between individuals.2 Blood levels then decline quickly — the half-life is around 40–60 minutes.3

Two practical consequences:

  • A "10 mg" gummy doesn't deliver 10 mg to your bloodstream — most of it never arrives.
  • Whatever does arrive, arrives early in the night. By the early-morning hours, oral melatonin has mostly cleared.

Practical dosage guidelines

  • Start with a consistent routine. Apply or take your melatonin at the same time nightly, 30–60 minutes before bed. Consistency trains your rhythm; sporadic use fights it.
  • If you're new to melatonin, it's reasonable to start at the lower end with an oral product, or simply try one patch and judge how you feel over a week — a single night is a poor test.
  • Don't stack products. Combining a gummy, a "PM" pain reliever and a patch multiplies doses unpredictably. Pick one.
  • Skip alcohol as a sleep aid. It fragments the second half of the night — the very problem you're trying to fix.

When to check with a doctor first

Melatonin has a good safety profile in adults, but talk to a healthcare provider before use if you're pregnant or nursing, take blood thinners, immunosuppressants, diabetes or blood-pressure medication, or manage a seizure disorder. Melatonin supplements are not recommended for children except under medical guidance. And if sleeplessness persists for weeks despite good habits, see a professional — chronic insomnia deserves proper evaluation, not just higher doses.
