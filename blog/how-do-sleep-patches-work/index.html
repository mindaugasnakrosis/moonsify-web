How Do Sleep Patches Work? Transdermal Sleep Support, Explained

By the Moonsify Team · Published August 13, 2026 · 6 min read

Sleep patches are one of the fastest-growing alternatives to pills, gummies and drops — but if you've never used one, the idea can seem almost too simple: stick a small patch on your skin before bed, sleep, peel it off in the morning. So what's actually happening underneath?

Key takeaways

  • Sleep patches use transdermal delivery — ingredients pass through the skin gradually instead of being swallowed and digested.
  • Release is slow and steady across the night, rather than the single spike you get from a pill or gummy.
  • Apply to clean, dry skin about 30 minutes before bed; remove in the morning.
  • Patches skip the digestive system entirely — no water, no sugar, nothing to swallow.

What is transdermal delivery?

"Transdermal" simply means through the skin. It's a well-established delivery method — nicotine patches for quitting smoking and motion-sickness patches have used it for decades, and researchers describe it as an attractive alternative to oral delivery precisely because it's gradual and bypasses the digestive tract.1

A sleep patch is a thin adhesive layer infused with active ingredients. Once it's in contact with warm skin, those ingredients begin to diffuse across the outer skin barrier in small, continuous amounts for as long as the patch stays on.

Why slow release matters for sleep

When you swallow a melatonin pill or gummy, the dose moves through your stomach and liver and enters your bloodstream in one relatively quick wave. Melatonin also has a short half-life — on the order of about 40 minutes to an hour2 — which is one reason some people fall asleep fine but find themselves staring at the ceiling at 3 a.m. once the dose has faded.

A patch works on a different curve. Because ingredients keep diffusing through the skin hour after hour, delivery is spread across the night instead of front-loaded at bedtime. For people whose problem is staying asleep rather than just falling asleep, that steady profile is the main appeal.

What's inside a Moonsify sleep patch

Each Moonsify patch combines four ingredients with long track records in sleep research and traditional use:

  • Melatonin (7 mg) — the hormone your brain naturally produces at night to signal that it's time to sleep. A meta-analysis of 19 randomized trials found supplemental melatonin helped people fall asleep faster and modestly increased total sleep time.3
  • Magnesium malate (41.32 mg) — a mineral involved in relaxing the nervous system; low magnesium status has been linked with poorer sleep. More on magnesium and sleep here.
  • Valerian root (27 mg) — an herb used for centuries as a calming sleep aid, with systematic reviews suggesting it may improve sleep quality.4
  • Hops (16.53 mg) — the same plant used in brewing, traditionally paired with valerian for its calming properties. See what the research says.

How to apply a sleep patch (the right way)

  1. Pick your spot. Choose an area of skin that's clean, dry and relatively hair-free — the upper arm, shoulder, or inner wrist all work well.
  2. Apply about 30 minutes before bed. Press the patch on firmly for a few seconds so the adhesive seals to the skin.
  3. Sleep. The patch works quietly through the night — it's thin enough that most people forget it's there.
  4. Remove in the morning. Peel it off and discard. Rotate to a slightly different spot the next night to keep skin happy.

A few practical tips: avoid applying lotion or oil to the area first (it interferes with adhesion), and don't apply a patch over broken or irritated skin.

Sleep patches vs. pills, gummies and drops

The core differences come down to delivery and convenience:

  • No swallowing or digestion — helpful if pills make you queasy or you're avoiding late-night sugar (gummies typically contain it).
  • Steady release instead of a single dose spike.
  • Travel-friendly — a pack of patches is flat, light, and TSA-proof.
